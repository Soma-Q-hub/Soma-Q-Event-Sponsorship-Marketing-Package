The plan on one page

One message, one action, two paths.

The booth does three things in order: stops a person walking past, starts a real conversation, and captures a contact that can be followed up within a day. Everything printed serves one of those jobs, or it is not printed. The event is Saturday, October 24, 2026.

GoalsGrow the email list and the contact list (both, with consent), and book free discovery calls from the best conversations.
AudienceAdults only. High-performing leaders, mostly men at this event, plus owners and HR or team leads. You are a regular sponsor, so some visitors already know you.
Budget$500 to $1,500 for printing and swag combined, tiered so the essentials fit and the rest is optional.

The rules this package follows

  • The big pieces tell the story; the table takes the scan. The banners and backdrop carry the question, what Soma-Q does, the loop and how to interrupt it, and the outcomes. They carry no QR codes. The counter sign, the cards and the leave-behind carry the scans.
  • A stranger can say what you do after five seconds. Every large piece states it in plain words, in dark type on a light field.
  • Outcomes lead. Every headline is an outcome or a question about one; mechanism and proof follow.
  • Four free first steps, always the same four. Burnout Signal Check, the free ebook, the weekly reset, and a free discovery call, each tagged so we can see which one people choose.
  • Light fields, deep type. Large prints use Linen with Deep Teal type and a small band of Deep Teal for the logo, because large solid dark fields reproduce less reliably and read poorly under venue lighting (see the print and lighting section).
  • Earn the swag. A card for everyone who talks with you; anything premium only for people who give a contact or book a call.
  • Capture during the conversation, track every piece. Each QR carries its own tag so the second booth can be better than the first.

Start from the stranger

Most people walking by have never heard of this.

A man at a summit, between sessions, coffee in hand, has not heard the phrase "nervous system alignment" and has no reason to care about it yet. He does know what a short fuse feels like, what three hours of bad sleep does to a Tuesday, and what it is to be good at the job and tired of it. The booth earns his attention in his words first, and introduces ours only after he is already nodding.

Strategy

The conversion path.

Five steps, each with one job and one owner. The diagram shows where each printed piece and each tool sits.

Booth anatomy: where each piece goes

Everything that must be read sits above table height (30 in): the lower part of a banner or backdrop is hidden by the table and by people. Stand beside the table, not behind it; people approach open space more readily than a barrier. That is common booth practice, not a measured finding.

Written for how this audience talks

Design stays neutral so the booth reads as inviting to anyone, and the language does the work of connecting with men: direct sentences, the physical and practical signals (sleep, tension, heavy decisions, a short fuse), and the framing of pressure as something trainable. There is no self-care, glow or journey vocabulary on any piece. If the event data later shows an audience that is overwhelmingly men, a second card with one men-focused headline is a small, low-risk addition, and I would test it against the neutral card rather than replace it.

At the booth

Conversation script and follow-up.

Open with a question, not a pitch

  • "What part of your week runs you down the most?"
  • "When the pressure spikes, what do you notice first: your body, your head, or your temper?"
  • "Is this something you are dealing with yourself, or for a team you lead?"

Then, in this order

  1. Listen for one specific cost (sleep, decisions, a short fuse, energy by mid-afternoon).
  2. Name it back in one plain sentence, tied to the outcome: restored energy, focused clarity, joy under pressure.
  3. Offer the next step that fits: the quiz for an individual, the overview for an organization.
  4. Capture it right there: "Tap my phone and I will send it to you now."
  5. Give the card. If it is a real conversation, offer the keepable and book the call on the spot.

Stay inside the scope

Soma-Q is an education and training practice. In conversation: do not diagnose, do not promise results ("clients report" and "designed to" are the verbs), and do not describe the work as therapy or treatment. If someone describes a medical or mental health crisis, listen, say this is outside what you do, and point them to a licensed provider.

QR codes

  • All codes are vector, Deep Teal on white, error correction level M, with a quiet zone. Do not recolor, stretch or place over imagery.
  • Keep every code at or above the size supplied. Codes are listed with their addresses in output/qr_urls.txt.
  • The default mode points every code to a stable soma-q.com/go/ address you can redirect later; the redirect list for your designer is in output/redirects_for_designer.md. If those routes are not live before printing, rebuild in direct mode.

Proof checklist before approving any print order

  • Search each PDF for the character "[": no placeholder may print.
  • Official logo, at full resolution, on every piece; colors match on the printed proof.
  • Every QR scanned from the printed proof on two different phones.
  • No text within 0.375 in of trim (0.25 in on the cards). Fonts embedded.
  • No em dashes, no coaching or clinical language, trademark marks present.

Formatting checks already run

Every piece was rendered and checked by a script for text running outside its sheet, text overlapping other text or a QR code, text clipped by its box, content running into the call-to-action block, a piece spilling onto a second sheet, leftover placeholders, and dash characters. I also looked at every page by eye. The script caught the leave-behind running under its call-to-action and the counter sign spilling to a second page; both are fixed. It cannot judge taste, so please look at the gallery yourself.
